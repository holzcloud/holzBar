# Privacy and permissions

## Principles

Every change to holzBar follows four rules — without taking a feature away:

- **Modern** — written the way a macOS app is written in 2026: current Swift, Swift concurrency and current SwiftUI and AppKit APIs. Outdated APIs are replaced as the code is touched.
- **Lean and fast** — as little CPU, energy, memory and disk as possible; no polling where macOS sends an event; a small app that launches fast.
- **Private** — holzBar never connects to the network: no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports, no update checks, no remote content. The only exception is a link you click, which opens in your browser. Your data stays on your Mac and out of the logs; the scripts folder, the approvals and the rules that use a script stay on the Mac in a file only you can read. To show hidden items when the network drops, holzBar only watches whether a network path is available (Apple's NWPathMonitor); it never opens a connection. The `no-network` check fails every pull request that adds networking code or a third-party package, and every build checks the app's binaries and entitlements for network access.
- **Least privilege** — holzBar asks only for the permissions a feature really needs, when it needs them, and says why.

Where holzBar doesn't meet a rule yet, that is a bug to fix.

## Permissions

Everything holzBar asks macOS for, the feature that needs it and when it is asked:

| Permission or entitlement | Needed for | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility** <sub>required</sub> | Reading where menu bar items are; moving, showing and clicking them for you; noticing clicks, scrolls and hovers in the menu bar for show on click, scroll and hover; on macOS 27, an Escape key press sent to Notification Center or Control Centre to close a panel holzBar opened; while **Notifications** in the Notch hub is on, reading the text of the banners Notification Center shows; while **Drag in the Menu Bar** is on (macOS 26 and 27; off by default), holding back a left click on an app's menu bar item until it is clear whether you click or drag it, then replaying it (the left mouse button only; no key is read); on macOS 27, while **Set the section with ⌘ Command-drag** is on (the default), noticing a left press with ⌘ Command held and its release, to read where an item came to rest after a drag (nothing is posted, no key is read and nothing is stored); on macOS 27, only on your action in Layout (Apply, or a move while Apply changes immediately is on), posting a ⌘ Command-drag at an item's position, one at a time, with the pointer hidden for a moment and put back; and, when you click the Shelf tile of a hidden system item (clock, Control Center, Wi-Fi, battery), a click or an Accessibility press on that item. None of this adds a permission | Asked on the first launch, in the Permissions window, which lists only Accessibility and Screen Recording |
| **Screen Recording** <sub>optional</sub> | Pictures of menu bar items in the holzBar Shelf, the search and the Layout pane (on macOS 27 taken once per item), and a moving wallpaper beside a menu bar shape (before macOS 27; other wallpapers are read from their file) | Offered, never required: Settings → Advanced and the first-launch Permissions window have **Grant…** and **Revoke…** for it, a confirmation in holzBar's own look asks once the first time you open the holzBar Shelf (holzBar remembers your answer), and the Layout pane and a menu bar shape with a moving wallpaper offer it where they need it. Replacement items are icons of holzBar's own that open an Apple item through Accessibility, as item hotkeys do. holzBar stores which system items you hid and your replacements' symbols and item keys on this Mac, in the settings; nothing is logged with a name. While a system item is hidden, Control Centre's camera and microphone indicator is not shown (see the note below) | Only while the module is on (Settings → Modules; off by default; macOS 27 only) |
| **Notch hub** <sub>no permission</sub> | One black shape that grows out of the notch, for optional modules. While the hub and "Open on hover" are on, holzBar reads the pointer's position from a global mouse-moved monitor (no permission needed), compares it with the notch's area and keeps and logs nothing; while the hub is open, a mouse-click monitor closes it when you click outside. Whether the screen is mirrored or shared is read once, when the hub would open. Nothing is stored but the hub's three settings (show what is active first, open on hover, displays) and the module you chose last, which stays on this Mac. | Only while the Notch hub module is on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Timers** <sub>no permission</sub> | The timers you start in the Notch hub: a length or a moment, the timer's state and your label are kept in holzBar's settings on this Mac only (never exported) so that timers survive a restart. While at least one timer runs, holzBar waits for the next end with one task and listens for sleep and wake, clock changes and time-zone changes; with no timer running it listens to nothing. The finish sound is a system sound; no system notification is sent and no permission is asked. Labels are never written to the log. | Only while the Timers module is on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Calendar** <sub>optional</sub> | Reading the events of today and tomorrow (title, time, calendar colour, location, notes and link, for the Join button) from the calendars macOS lists, to show them in the notch hub. The text is held in memory while the hub shows it and never stored, logged, exported or sent; which calendars you switched off stays on this Mac, and holzBar reads the calendars' names only while the module's settings are open. The Join button opens a link only when you click it. The signature carries one entitlement, `com.apple.security.personal-information.calendars`, which macOS requires of a hardened app before it will ask; it grants nothing by itself. With the hub and the module off, holzBar creates no event store, listens to nothing and waits for nothing | Asked when the Calendar module and the Notch hub are on and macOS has not been asked yet; Grant… and Revoke… are also in Settings → Advanced (the first-launch Permissions window does not list it), and System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars changes it. Without it the tab says so and nothing else is affected |
| **Live activities** <sub>optional</sub> | The notch widens while something is going on, and the module lists it in the Notch hub. Camera and microphone in use by another app: holzBar reads only whether a process records audio (Core Audio) and whether a camera is running (CoreMediaIO), on a background queue, exactly as for the dot on its icon (macOS 14.2 and later); it opens no audio or video and needs no permission. Downloads: while its switch is on, holzBar watches the entries of your Downloads folder, without polling, and reads the names of unfinished downloads (the marks of Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox and Opera) to show them in the open hub; it never reads what you download. macOS asks once for access to the Downloads folder, with holzBar's own sentence. File names stay in memory while an activity is shown and are never written to a log or a file, never exported or sent, and never shown in the widened notch. | Only while the Live activities module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default). The camera and microphone source needs no permission; the Downloads source has its own switch (off by default) and is asked when you switch it on. Refuse or revoke it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Files & Folders; without it the module's row says so and nothing asks again by itself |
| **File drop** <sub>no permission</sub> | The files you drag onto the Notch hub: holzBar keeps each file's location in memory only, shows its name and a type icon, and forgets it when you quit holzBar or switch the module off; it does not open, read, copy or move the files, makes no previews and writes no name to the log. A tile you drag out is copied by the app or folder you drop it on, under that app's own access to the folder. To open the hub for a file drag, holzBar notices mouse presses, drags and releases in other apps (no permission is needed; it keeps and logs nothing) and, once per drag, asks the drag whether it holds files; that exists only while the hub, the module and "Open when you drag a file to the notch" are on. While the hub opens it checks whether each stashed file still exists. | Only while the File drop module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Display controls** <sub>no permission</sub> | The volume and brightness sliders of the Notch hub. Volume: holzBar reads and sets the volume and mute of the default sound output with public Core Audio calls and, only while the hub is open, listens for changes of the volume, the mute and the output device. Names of outputs and displays are shown in the open hub, kept in memory and never written to the log, stored, exported or sent. Brightness: a macOS interface that Apple does not document (DisplayServices) is loaded on first use, only while "Brightness" in the module's settings is on, to read and set the brightness of the displays macOS can change; it is looked up at run time and not linked, it needs no permission, and holzBar switches it off by itself, and says so in the hub, if macOS has changed it. It may stop working in a later macOS. External monitors (DDC): a macOS interface that Apple does not document (IOAVService, on Apple silicon) sets the brightness, contrast, volume and mute of external monitors over DDC/CI; it is off by default, looked up at run time and not linked, only while "External monitors (DDC)" in the module's settings is on and the hub is open, it needs no permission and no entitlement, display names stay in memory and are never logged, stored, exported or sent, and holzBar switches it off by itself, and says so in the hub, if macOS has changed it or it fails. | Only while the Display controls module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default). Brightness is read when the hub opens and after each of your changes; the switch turns the call off. The monitors' values are read when the Display tab appears (or when the hub opens on the Overview with a monitor's brightness) and after each of your changes; "External monitors (DDC)" turns the call off |
| **Notifications** <sub>uses Accessibility</sub> | The banners and alerts macOS shows, in the Notifications module of the Notch hub. While the module and the hub are on, holzBar watches Notification Center's windows through Accessibility and reads the app name (when it can), title and text of each banner or alert that appears; it reads nothing between banners and does not open macOS's notification database. The last twenty are kept in memory only: never stored, exported, logged or sent, not recorded while the screen is mirrored or shared, never recorded for an app you excluded (that list of names stays on this Mac, is in no export and is the only thing stored), and cleared when you switch the module or the hub off or quit holzBar. This module needs and asks no Notifications permission (only the optional System notifications of System alerts does; see the row Notifications permission). holzBar sees only what macOS shows on screen as a banner or alert, and it follows the structure Notification Center has on macOS 27.2; if macOS changes what Notification Center shows to Accessibility, the module says it is not reading | Only while the Notifications module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Now Playing** <sub>no permission</sub> | Reads the playback notifications that Music and Spotify send to every app on this Mac (title, artist, album, state, and Spotify's position), shows them in the Notch hub and keeps them in memory only. The play, pause, next and previous buttons post a media key with the Accessibility access holzBar already has, only when you click one. The artwork is read from the song's file on this Mac; macOS may ask once for a folder (Desktop, Documents, Downloads, iCloud Drive, an external volume) when a song is stored there, and a symbol is shown if you refuse. Nothing about a song is stored, logged, exported or sent; the only stored values are which players it follows and the Read all apps switch, which stays on this Mac. **Read all apps** (off by default) reads the title, artist and app name of what any app plays through a private macOS interface that Apple does not document, in a library of holzBar's own that Apple's `perl` loads and that holzBar starts only while the hub is open and the switch is on. It needs no permission of its own (on macOS 27.2, where holzBar measured it, macOS asked for nothing), opens no network connection and reads no file, and it switches itself off for the session, and says so, if macOS has changed the interface | Only while the Now Playing module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default), and for Read all apps also its switch, and only while the hub is open |
| **System readouts** <sub>no permission</sub> | The tiles of the Notch hub's Overview and System readouts tab, and the readout sources of the Widgets module. It reads, from macOS on this Mac only, the processor load per core, the memory use, swap and pressure, the byte counters of the Wi-Fi and Ethernet interfaces (no Wi-Fi query, no network name, no address and no traffic content), the disk read and write counters and the free space, the battery charge, the time left and the power adapter's watts, and the thermal state. It needs no permission and no entitlement. Nothing is stored but the settings (which tiles are on, the refresh interval, the speed unit and the Read GPU load switch); the two-minute curves stay in memory and are gone when holzBar quits; nothing is logged and nothing is sent. **Read GPU load** (off by default) reads how busy the graphics processor is from a statistic of the graphics driver, through a private macOS interface that Apple does not document and that can stop working. It has its own switch, labelled as a private interface in Settings, stored on this Mac only and never exported or imported; it needs no permission, opens no network connection, reads nothing but that one number, and switches itself off for the session, and says so, if macOS has changed the interface or a read fails three times in a row | Only while the System readouts module is on (Settings → Modules, under System monitor; off by default) and its tiles are shown in the open hub, or while a readout widget is visible; never while the bar item is hidden, the hub is closed, or the Mac is asleep or locked. Read GPU load also only while its switch is on |
| **System alerts** <sub>no permission</sub> | Alerts in the notch when the CPU load, the memory pressure, the thermal state, the free disk space or the Mac battery cross a limit you set. It reads those values from macOS on this Mac only, through the same shared monitor as System readouts and only for the rules that are on, and compares them with your limits; the values stay in memory and are never stored, logged or sent. Nothing is stored but the module's settings (which rules are on, their limits and times, and the System notifications switch). An alert is a short text of holzBar's own with the rule's name and value, shown in the notch; it names no app, file or device. It needs no permission and no entitlement | Only while the System alerts module is on (Settings → Modules, under System monitor; off by default) and at least one rule is on; with the module off, or with every rule off, nothing is measured, nothing waits and nothing listens. With the Mac asleep or locked nothing is read, and an alert never fires in the 30 seconds after the Mac wakes |
| **Notifications permission** <sub>optional</sub> | System notifications of System alerts: when it is switched on, each alert that fires is also a macOS notification, so holzBar needs your permission to post one. holzBar asks for the alert option only (no sound, no badge, no other option), posts one notification per rule, holding the rule's name and value and nothing else, and removes it when the condition clears or when the switch or the module goes off. The switch is stored on this Mac only and is in no export or import. Nothing about a notification leaves the Mac | Asked by macOS once, only when you switch **System notifications** on (Settings → Modules → System alerts); never at launch, never by the module itself, never by **Test alert**, and not while the switch is off. If you refuse, the module says "Notifications are off for holzBar" with a button that opens System Settings → Notifications, the alerts in the notch go on, and nothing asks again by itself. | **Widgets** <sub>no permission</sub> | Text of your own in the menu bar from sources that need no permission: the date and time, the battery (with the time left and the adapter's watts), CPU load (also per core cluster), memory (also pressure and swap), uptime, free disk space, the network speed, the disk activity and the thermal state, the lowest battery level of your connected accessories (Lowest Device Battery, offered while Read battery levels is on; see the Accessory battery levels row below), and, while Read GPU load is on, the GPU load (see the System readouts row above), read from macOS on this Mac only while a widget is visible, never while it is hidden or while the Mac is asleep or locked. | **Scripts** | A script you allowed runs as you, with holzBar's permissions (Accessibility, and Screen Recording if you allowed it); macOS asks separately before it controls another app. An allowed script may be able to use holzBar's own Accessibility and Screen Recording grants, so treat it as being as powerful as holzBar. holzBar gives a script only the event name (`HOLZBAR_EVENT`, one of six fixed names) and keeps only the first line of its output, in memory. | **Accessory battery levels** <sub>no permission</sub> | The levels of the accessories (AirPods, keyboards, mice) that macOS lists among its power sources, through one macOS call that Apple does not document (`IOPSCopyPowerSourcesByType`, looked up when it is needed), so it may stop working in a later macOS; if it is missing, the Accessory alerts module says that levels are not available and nothing else is affected. | **AppleScript** <sub>no permission of holzBar</sub> | holzBar answers the commands of its dictionary (show, hide, apply a profile, switch rules and Zen mode). macOS asks the calling app (Script Editor, a Shortcut) for permission to control holzBar; holzBar sends no Apple events itself | Only when a script calls holzBar |
| **Usage counters** <sub>off by default</sub> | One file, `~/Library/Application Support/holzBar/Usage.json`, readable only by you and excluded from backups: how often each visible item was opened per day for 30 days. No times, titles or order. Never exported, logged or put into diagnostics; "View Data…" shows it, and "Forget everything…" or turning suggestions off (which asks first) deletes it | Only when you turn on usage suggestions |
| **Entitlements** | One, `com.apple.security.personal-information.calendars`, which a hardened app needs before macOS will let it ask for Calendar access. It lets macOS ask and nothing more: you are still asked, and you can revoke the access in System Settings or in holzBar's Settings → Advanced. There is no `get-task-allow`, which would let a debugger attach; from 0.0.7-beta2 to 0.0.13 holzBar carried no entitlement at all. holzBar runs without the App Sandbox, because Accessibility event taps and the menu bar's private WindowServer calls do not work in it, and it has no network entitlement | — |
| **Location Services** | Only for the automation condition "Wi-Fi network named …": since macOS 14 the system gives the network name only to apps that may use Location Services. holzBar reads that name and requests no location updates, and stores no location. Without it the condition is never true | Only when you add that condition: holzBar explains why first, then macOS asks |
| **Info.plist usage strings** | The ones for Location Services, for Bluetooth, for Calendar and for the Downloads folder above. Accessibility and Screen Recording need none | — |
| **Reset and Grant Again** | Runs `tccutil reset` for holzBar's own entry only, when a stale permission keeps the permissions window open; shown under a permission's row in the Permissions window while that permission is missing | Only when you click it |
| **Undo history** <sub>no permission</sub> | The last 20 changes of the layout, so the undo notice, ⌘Z and the Undo button in Layout can take them back. Kept in memory only: never stored, exported or logged, and gone when holzBar quits | Only after a change of the layout |

holzBar is one app. Its only nested code is the library for Read all apps, `Contents/Frameworks/NowPlayingRemote.framework`, which is signed with it, carries no entitlement and is used only while that switch is on and the hub is open; it has no XPC service or helper app, and CI and the release check that nothing else is nested. Apart from that library, no code that ships with holzBar runs with these permissions besides holzBar itself; a script that you allowed is your own code and runs as described in the Scripts row. Accessibility calls into other apps, such as finding the app behind each menu bar item on macOS 26 and registering the observers that notice new and changed items, run off the main thread with time limits, so a slow app does not stall holzBar or your clicks.

Without any permission, holzBar also reads, on your Mac only:

- **Code signatures of running apps** (macOS 26), to tell macOS's own processes from other apps when it works out which app a menu bar item belongs to.
- **The category an app declares** in its own `Info.plist` (and its bundle identifier), to sort menu bar items for Tidy Up and Smart Categories; read on your Mac for the open sheet or search, never stored or sent. With Smart Categories off, the search reads none.
- **Which apps are running** and **their names and icons**, for the holzBar Shelf's closed apps (off by default): as the Shelf opens, holzBar compares the apps it remembers a hidden item for with the running apps and shows the ones that are not running dimmed. Read on your Mac for the open Shelf, never stored or sent. With **Show apps that are not running** off, it reads none of it.


> [!WARNING]
> **macOS 27: the camera, microphone and screen recording indicator.** While holzBar hides menu bar items on macOS 27, or a system item with Apple Items, Control Centre does not show its privacy indicator — green for the camera, orange for the microphone, indigo for screen sharing or recording. The small green dot beside the clock still appears while the camera is on. The indicator comes back while holzBar hides no item. holzBar cannot prevent this: macOS removes the indicator whenever an app hides items the way holzBar must on macOS 27. Instead, holzBar puts a dot on its own icon while it hides items and another app uses the microphone (orange) or a camera (green), and shows its icon for that even when the icon is set to "No icon". Screen recording is not covered: no public API reports it. The setting is in **Settings → General**, on by default, and needs no permission.
