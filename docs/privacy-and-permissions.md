# Privacy and permissions

## Principles

Every change to holzBar follows four rules — without taking a feature away:

- **Modern** — written the way a macOS app is written in 2026: current Swift, Swift concurrency and current SwiftUI and AppKit APIs. Outdated APIs are replaced as the code is touched.
- **Lean and fast** — as little CPU, energy, memory and disk as possible; no polling where macOS sends an event; a small app that launches fast.
- **Private** — holzBar never connects to the network: no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports, no update checks, no remote content. The only exception is a link you click, which opens in your browser. Your data stays on your Mac and out of the logs; the scripts folder, the approvals and the rules that use a script stay on the Mac in a file only you can read. To show hidden items when the network drops, holzBar only watches whether a network path is available (Apple's NWPathMonitor); it never opens a connection. The `no-network` check fails every pull request that adds networking code or a third-party package, and every build checks the app's binaries and entitlements for network access.
- **Least privilege** — holzBar asks only for the permissions a feature really needs, when it needs them, and says why.

Where holzBar doesn't meet a rule yet, that is a bug to fix.

## Permissions

Everything holzBar asks macOS for, the feature that needs it and when it is asked:

| Permission or entitlement | Needed for | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility** <sub>required</sub> | Reading where menu bar items are; moving, showing and clicking them for you; noticing clicks, scrolls and hovers in the menu bar for show on click, scroll and hover; on macOS 27, an Escape key press sent to Notification Center or Control Centre to close a panel holzBar opened; while **Notifications** in the Notch hub is on, reading the text of the banners Notification Center shows; while **Drag in the Menu Bar** is on (macOS 26 and 27; off by default), holding back a left click on an app's menu bar item until it is clear whether you click or drag it, then replaying it (the left mouse button only; no key is read) | Asked on the first launch |
| **Screen Recording** <sub>optional</sub> | Pictures of menu bar items in the holzBar Shelf, the search and the Menu Bar Layout pane (on macOS 27 taken once per item), and a moving wallpaper beside a menu bar shape (before macOS 27; other wallpapers are read from their file) | Offered, never required: Settings → Advanced and the first-launch Permissions window have Grant and Revoke buttons for it, a window asks once the first time you open the holzBar Shelf (holzBar remembers your answer), and the Menu Bar Layout pane and a menu bar shape with a moving wallpaper offer it where they need it. Without it, everything else works, the holzBar Shelf and the search show app icons, and nothing captures the screen |
| **Login item** | Starting holzBar when you log in | Only when you turn on "Launch at login" |
| **Microphone and camera use** <sub>no permission</sub> | The dot on holzBar's icon (macOS 27) while another app uses the microphone or a camera, and the automation conditions "camera in use" and "microphone in use" (macOS 14.2 and later). holzBar reads only whether a process records audio (Core Audio) and whether a camera is running (CoreMediaIO), on a background queue; it opens no audio or video, and records and stores nothing | Only while "Show a dot on the holzBar icon…" is on (Settings → General, on by default, macOS 27), or while an enabled rule has one of the two conditions |
| **Accessibility display options** <sub>no permission</sub> | Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast: System Glass, the holzBar Shelf and Settings' groups are drawn opaque with a border. Reduce Motion: items in Menu Bar Layout jump instead of sliding. holzBar reads the options from macOS (`NSWorkspace` and the SwiftUI environment) and stores nothing | Only while System Glass is the tint in use or the holzBar Shelf is open (one change notification, no polling), and while a Settings view or Menu Bar Layout shows them |
| **Displays** <sub>no permission</sub> | Per display, hidden items can be always shown or collapsed (Settings → General). holzBar reads the pointer's position from a global mouse-moved monitor to know which display it is on; it stores only the choice per display identifier, in the settings, and nothing is logged with a display name | Only while at least one display has a choice other than Default; with none, no monitor exists |
| **Apple Items** <sub>no permission</sub> | Hiding the clock, Control Center, Wi-Fi or the battery on macOS 27 uses the call holzBar already makes to hide applications' items, with a shorter list of the system items it keeps. Replacement items are icons of holzBar's own that open an Apple item through Accessibility, as item hotkeys do. holzBar stores which system items you hid and your replacements' symbols and item keys on this Mac, in the settings; nothing is logged with a name. While a system item is hidden, Control Centre's camera and microphone indicator is not shown (see the note below) | Only while the module is on (Settings → Modules; off by default; macOS 27 only) |
| **Notch hub** <sub>no permission</sub> | A panel at the notch for optional modules. While the hub and "Open on hover" are on, holzBar reads the pointer's position from a global mouse-moved monitor (no permission needed), compares it with the notch's area and keeps and logs nothing; while the hub is open, a mouse-click monitor closes it when you click outside. Whether the screen is mirrored or shared is read once, when the hub would open. Nothing is stored but the hub's two settings (open on hover, displays) and the tab it showed last, which stays on this Mac. | Only while the Notch hub module is on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Timers** <sub>no permission</sub> | The timers you start in the Notch hub: a length or a moment, the timer's state and your label are kept in holzBar's settings on this Mac only (never exported) so that timers survive a restart. While at least one timer runs, holzBar waits for the next end with one task and listens for sleep and wake, clock changes and time-zone changes; with no timer running it listens to nothing. The finish sound is a system sound; no system notification is sent and no permission is asked. Labels are never written to the log. | Only while the Timers module is on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Calendar** <sub>optional</sub> | Reading the events of today and tomorrow (title, time, calendar colour, location, notes and link, for the Join button) from the calendars macOS lists, to show them in the notch hub. The text is held in memory while the hub shows it and never stored, logged, exported or sent; which calendars you switched off stays on this Mac, and holzBar reads the calendars' names only while the module's settings are open. The Join button opens a link only when you click it. The signature carries one entitlement, `com.apple.security.personal-information.calendars`, which macOS requires of a hardened app before it will ask; it grants nothing by itself. With the hub and the module off, holzBar creates no event store, listens to nothing and waits for nothing | Asked when the Calendar module and the Notch hub are on and macOS has not been asked yet; Grant and Revoke are also in Settings → Advanced and in the Permissions window, and System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars changes it. Without it the tab says so and nothing else is affected |
| **Live activities** <sub>optional</sub> | A capsule at the notch and a tab in the Notch hub for what is going on. Camera and microphone in use by another app: holzBar reads only whether a process records audio (Core Audio) and whether a camera is running (CoreMediaIO), on a background queue, exactly as for the dot on its icon (macOS 14.2 and later); it opens no audio or video and needs no permission. Downloads: while its switch is on, holzBar watches the entries of your Downloads folder, without polling, and reads the names of unfinished downloads (the marks of Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox and Opera) to show them in the open hub; it never reads what you download. macOS asks once for access to the Downloads folder, with holzBar's own sentence. File names stay in memory while an activity is shown and are never written to a log or a file, never exported or sent, and never shown in the capsule. | Only while the Live activities module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default). The camera and microphone source needs no permission; the Downloads source has its own switch (off by default) and is asked when you switch it on. Refuse or revoke it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Files & Folders; without it the module's row says so and nothing asks again by itself |
| **File drop** <sub>no permission</sub> | The files you drag onto the Notch hub: holzBar keeps each file's location in memory only, shows its name and a type icon, and forgets it when you quit holzBar or switch the module off; it does not open, read, copy or move the files, makes no previews and writes no name to the log. A tile you drag out is copied by the app or folder you drop it on, under that app's own access to the folder. To open the hub for a file drag, holzBar notices mouse presses, drags and releases in other apps (no permission is needed; it keeps and logs nothing) and, once per drag, asks the drag whether it holds files; that exists only while the hub, the module and "Open when you drag a file to the notch" are on. While the hub opens it checks whether each stashed file still exists. | Only while the File drop module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Display controls** <sub>no permission</sub> | The volume and brightness sliders of the Notch hub. Volume: holzBar reads and sets the volume and mute of the default sound output with public Core Audio calls and, only while the hub is open, listens for changes of the volume, the mute and the output device. Names of outputs and displays are shown in the open tab, kept in memory and never written to the log, stored, exported or sent. Brightness: a macOS interface that Apple does not document (DisplayServices) is loaded on first use, only while "Brightness" in the module's settings is on, to read and set the brightness of the displays macOS can change; it is looked up at run time and not linked, it needs no permission, and holzBar switches it off by itself, and says so in the tab, if macOS has changed it. It may stop working in a later macOS. | Only while the Display controls module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default). Brightness is read when the hub opens and after each of your changes; the switch turns the call off |
| **Notifications** <sub>uses Accessibility</sub> | The banners and alerts macOS shows, in the Notifications tab of the Notch hub. While the module and the hub are on, holzBar watches Notification Center's windows through Accessibility and reads the app name (when it can), title and text of each banner or alert that appears; it reads nothing between banners and does not open macOS's notification database. The last twenty are kept in memory only: never stored, exported, logged or sent, not recorded while the screen is mirrored or shared, never recorded for an app you excluded (that list of names stays on this Mac, is in no export and is the only thing stored), and cleared when you switch the module or the hub off or quit holzBar. No Notifications permission is needed or asked. holzBar sees only what macOS shows on screen as a banner or alert, and it follows the structure Notification Center has on macOS 27.2; if macOS changes what Notification Center shows to Accessibility, the module says it is not reading | Only while the Notifications module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default) |
| **Now Playing** <sub>no permission</sub> | Reads the playback notifications that Music and Spotify send to every app on this Mac (title, artist, album, state, and Spotify's position), shows them in the Notch hub and keeps them in memory only. The play, pause, next and previous buttons post a media key with the Accessibility access holzBar already has, only when you click one. The artwork is read from the song's file on this Mac; macOS may ask once for a folder (Desktop, Documents, Downloads, iCloud Drive, an external volume) when a song is stored there, and a symbol is shown if you refuse. Nothing about a song is stored, logged, exported or sent; the only stored values are which players it follows and the Read all apps switch, which stays on this Mac. **Read all apps** (off by default) reads the title, artist and app name of what any app plays through a private macOS interface that Apple does not document, in a library of holzBar's own that Apple's `perl` loads and that holzBar starts only while the hub is open and the switch is on. It needs no permission of its own (on macOS 27.2, where holzBar measured it, macOS asked for nothing), opens no network connection and reads no file, and it switches itself off for the session, and says so, if macOS has changed the interface | Only while the Now Playing module and the Notch hub are on (Settings → Modules, under Notch; off by default), and for Read all apps also its switch, and only while the hub is open |
| **Widgets** <sub>no permission</sub> | Text of your own in the menu bar from sources that need no permission: the date and time, the battery, CPU load, memory, uptime and free disk space, read from macOS on this Mac only while a widget is visible, never while it is hidden or while the Mac is asleep or locked. Nothing is stored but the list of widgets (each one's source, format and order) and nothing is logged. A Shortcut button's Shortcut name stays on this Mac under a key of its own and is never exported or imported; the button opens the Shortcuts app with that name and nothing else, and no data goes to the Shortcut or comes back. The built-in items (Focus, AirDrop, media controls, fast user switching, a timer, app launchers) read nothing of your data: the media item posts the media-key events a keyboard posts, which uses the Accessibility access holzBar already has, and, only when you choose "Only while sound plays", asks Core Audio whether some app plays sound on the default output (no audio is opened and nothing is recorded); an app launcher stores the bundle identifier of the app you picked, and the timer keeps its countdown in memory. A script widget runs a script you allowed like any other, under the rules of the Scripts row below (only the folder you chose, only the exact content you allowed, no arguments, no shell, the event name `widget-refresh` as its only input, the first line of its output kept in memory and shown as plain text), only while it is visible, on a click and on system events, never on a timer. Which script a widget runs stays on this Mac in the scripts file and is never exported or imported | Only while the Widgets module is on (Settings → Modules; off by default) |
| **Layout snapshots** | Small files in `~/Library/Application Support/holzBar/Snapshots`, readable only by you, with the sections of your menu bar items and applications (bundle identifiers and item names). They are not exported, and "Delete All Snapshots" removes them | On by default; turn it off in Menu Bar Layout |
| **Scripts** | A script you allowed runs as you, with holzBar's permissions (Accessibility, and Screen Recording if you allowed it); macOS asks separately before it controls another app. An allowed script may be able to use holzBar's own Accessibility and Screen Recording grants, so treat it as being as powerful as holzBar. holzBar gives a script only the event name (`HOLZBAR_EVENT`, one of six fixed names) and keeps only the first line of its output, in memory. Nothing about scripts, their approvals or the rules that use them is exported or imported, and scripts run only from the folder you chose | Only when you allow a script and a rule, a profile hook or a script widget uses it |
| **Bluetooth** <sub>optional</sub> | The automation condition "A Bluetooth device is connected" and the Accessory alerts module: which of your paired devices are connected, from macOS's connect and disconnect notifications, read on this Mac. holzBar discovers, pairs with and connects to nothing and sends nothing. A rule stores only the name you pick; Accessory alerts keeps device names and addresses in memory while it runs and does not log, store, export or send them | Asked by macOS the first time you add such a condition, or the first time Accessory alerts runs (the module and the Notch hub are both on), after the module's row has said "Needs Bluetooth"; never at launch, never while the module is off, and never to connect, scan or pair. Grant and Revoke are also in Settings → Advanced and in the Permissions window, and System Settings → Privacy & Security → Bluetooth changes it. Without it the module says so and nothing asks again by itself |
| **Accessory battery levels** <sub>no permission</sub> | The levels of the accessories (AirPods, keyboards, mice) that macOS lists among its power sources, through one macOS call that Apple does not document (`IOPSCopyPowerSourcesByType`, looked up when it is needed), so it may stop working in a later macOS; if it is missing, the Accessories tab says that levels are not available and nothing else is affected. It asks for no permission, stores nothing, logs no name and sends nothing | Only while Accessory alerts runs and **Read battery levels** is on (Settings → Modules, under Notch, on by default); the switch turns the call off. Read when macOS reports a power-source change, when an accessory connects or disconnects and when the hub opens, plus one wait of five minutes while a connected accessory has a level |
| **AppleScript** <sub>no permission of holzBar</sub> | holzBar answers the commands of its dictionary (show, hide, apply a profile, switch rules and Zen mode). macOS asks the calling app (Script Editor, a Shortcut) for permission to control holzBar; holzBar sends no Apple events itself | Only when a script calls holzBar |
| **Usage counters** <sub>off by default</sub> | One file, `~/Library/Application Support/holzBar/Usage.json`, readable only by you and excluded from backups: how often each visible item was opened per day for 30 days. No times, titles or order. Never exported, logged or put into diagnostics; "View Data" shows it and "Forget Everything" or turning it off deletes it | Only when you turn on usage suggestions |
| **Entitlements** | One, `com.apple.security.personal-information.calendars`, which a hardened app needs before macOS will let it ask for Calendar access. It lets macOS ask and nothing more: you are still asked, and you can revoke the access in System Settings or holzBar's Permissions window. There is no `get-task-allow`, which would let a debugger attach; from 0.0.7-beta2 to 0.0.13 holzBar carried no entitlement at all. holzBar runs without the App Sandbox, because Accessibility event taps and the menu bar's private WindowServer calls do not work in it, and it has no network entitlement | — |
| **Location Services** | Only for the automation condition "Wi-Fi network named …": since macOS 14 the system gives the network name only to apps that may use Location Services. holzBar reads that name and requests no location updates, and stores no location. Without it the condition is never true | Only when you add that condition: holzBar explains why first, then macOS asks |
| **Info.plist usage strings** | The ones for Location Services, for Bluetooth, for Calendar and for the Downloads folder above. Accessibility and Screen Recording need none | — |
| **Reset and Grant Again** | Runs `tccutil reset` for holzBar's own entry only, when a stale permission keeps the permissions window open | Only when you click it |

holzBar is one app. Its only nested code is the library for Read all apps, `Contents/Frameworks/NowPlayingRemote.framework`, which is signed with it, carries no entitlement and is used only while that switch is on and the hub is open; it has no XPC service or helper app, and CI and the release check that nothing else is nested. Apart from that library, no code that ships with holzBar runs with these permissions besides holzBar itself; a script that you allowed is your own code and runs as described in the Scripts row. Accessibility calls into other apps, such as finding the app behind each menu bar item on macOS 26 and registering the observers that notice new and changed items, run off the main thread with time limits, so a slow app does not stall holzBar or your clicks.

Without any permission, holzBar also reads, on your Mac only:

- **Code signatures of running apps** (macOS 26), to tell macOS's own processes from other apps when it works out which app a menu bar item belongs to.
- **The category an app declares** in its own `Info.plist` (and its bundle identifier), to sort menu bar items for Tidy Up and Smart Categories; read on your Mac for the open sheet or search, never stored or sent. With Smart Categories off, the search reads none.
- **Which apps are running** and **their names and icons**, for the holzBar Shelf's closed apps (off by default): as the Shelf opens, holzBar compares the apps it remembers a hidden item for with the running apps and shows the ones that are not running dimmed. Read on your Mac for the open Shelf, never stored or sent. With **Show applications that are not running** off, it reads none of it.

> [!WARNING]
> **macOS 27: the camera, microphone and screen recording indicator.** While holzBar hides menu bar items on macOS 27, or a system item with Apple Items, Control Centre does not show its privacy indicator — green for the camera, orange for the microphone, indigo for screen sharing or recording. The small green dot beside the clock still appears while the camera is on. The indicator comes back while holzBar hides no item. holzBar cannot prevent this: macOS removes the indicator whenever an app hides items the way holzBar must on macOS 27. Instead, holzBar puts a dot on its own icon while it hides items and another app uses the microphone (orange) or a camera (green), and shows its icon for that even when the icon is set to "No icon". Screen recording is not covered: no public API reports it. The setting is in **Settings → General**, on by default, and needs no permission.
