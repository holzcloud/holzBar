## New in 1.2.0

1.2.0 is in beta; see the [release notes of 1.2.0 beta 1](release-notes/v1.2.0-beta1.md).

- **System readouts** (Settings → Modules → System monitor, an optional module that is off until you switch it on, needs no permission and measures nothing while it is off or while nothing it feeds is shown): tiles for the CPU, GPU, memory, network, disk and battery in the Notch hub's Overview and in a tab of their own.
  - **CPU** shows the load, the thermal state when the Mac runs warm (the **Thermal state** switch) and, on Apple silicon, the load of the performance and the efficiency cores (**P 42 % · E 12 %**). **GPU** shows how busy the graphics processor is; it appears only while **Read GPU load** is on. **Memory** shows the used share with one line under it: the memory pressure while it is not normal, otherwise the swap in use, otherwise the capacity. **Network** shows the download and upload speed. **Disk** shows the free space and the capacity (the read and write speed is the Disk Activity widget). **Battery** (on a Mac with a battery) shows the charge with the time left or until full and the power adapter's watts.
  - **Curves**: the CPU, GPU, memory and network tiles draw the last two minutes as a quiet curve. The curve lives in memory only and is gone when holzBar quits; with Increase Contrast it is drawn brighter and without the area.
  - **Settings**: a switch for every tile, **Thermal state**, **Refresh tiles every** (a choice of seconds) and **Network speed unit** (bytes or bits per second, which also changes the Network Speed widget).
  - **Read GPU load** is an optional switch, off by default and kept on this Mac only. It reads one statistic of the graphics driver through a private macOS interface that Apple does not document and that can stop working; holzBar switches it off by itself, and says so, if macOS changes it.
- **System alerts** (Settings → Modules → System monitor, an optional module that is off until you switch it on, needs no permission and measures nothing while it is off or while no rule is on): alerts in the notch when the Mac crosses a limit you set. Five rules, each with its own switch, limit and **Held for** time:
  - **CPU load** (on by default; above 90 percent for 5 minutes; 50 to 95 percent in steps of 5), **Memory pressure** (off; Warning, held for 1 minute; or Critical), **Thermal state** (on; Serious, with no wait; or Fair or Critical), **Free disk space** (on; below 10 GB for 1 minute; 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 or 200 GB) and **Mac battery** (on; below 20 percent, only on battery, with no wait; 5 to 50 percent; shown only on a Mac with a battery). **Held for** is Immediately, 10 or 30 seconds, 1, 5 or 15 minutes.
  - **How an alert shows**: the notch widens for about 30 seconds with the rule's symbol and value, amber near the limit and red well past it, and the alert stays as a pill beside the notch and in the **Live activities** list while the hub is open. It works without the Notch hub (at the top centre of the menu bar on a display without a notch, below the notch where the bar is too full to widen it). When the condition clears, the notch says "Normal" for a moment and the alert leaves.
  - **No flapping**: an alert fires only after the condition has held for the time you set, clears only after the value has moved back past the limit by a margin and stayed there for 30 seconds, repeats at most every 30 minutes for the same rule, and at most four alerts fire in an hour. Nothing fires in the 30 seconds after the Mac wakes, the screen unlocks or a display changes, or after you switch a rule on.
  - **Test alert** shows a sample alert for five seconds; with System notifications on and allowed it also sends one sample notification, which is removed again, and it never asks for the permission.
  - **System notifications** is an optional switch, off by default and kept on this Mac only. When you switch it on, macOS asks once for the Notifications permission (the alert option only, no sound and no badge); then every alert that fires is also a notification, one per rule, removed when the alert clears or when you switch the option off. The notification holds the rule's name and value, nothing else. If macOS refuses, the module says "Notifications are off for holzBar" with a button to System Settings, and the alerts in the notch go on.
- **Devices** (Settings → Modules → Notch → Accessory alerts, and the hub's **Accessories** tab): one row per device name, with a symbol, the battery level when macOS reports one (the lowest part for AirPods), connected devices first and paired devices that are not connected in grey. The Settings list shows how many are connected and needs no permission of its own: it reads the paired devices only when Bluetooth is already allowed, and Settings never asks for it. Battery levels use the **Read battery levels** switch of Accessory alerts, with the module and the Notch hub off as well.
- **Lowest Device Battery** (Settings → Modules → Widgets): a widget source that shows the symbol of the device kind and the lowest battery level of your connected accessories, such as AirPods, a keyboard or a mouse. The name of the device is only in the tooltip and for VoiceOver. It is offered while **Read battery levels** is on, works without the Notch hub and without Accessory alerts, and shows an en dash when no device reports a level. It refreshes when macOS reports a power-source change and every five minutes while the widget is visible.
- **New widget readouts** (Settings → Modules → Widgets): sources **Network Speed** (Download, Upload, or both), **Disk Activity** (Read, Write, or both), **GPU Load** (Percent; offered only while Read GPU load is on) and **Thermal State** (a word such as Nominal or Serious); new formats for the old sources: CPU Load **Per Core Cluster**, Memory **Pressure** and **Swap Used**, Battery **Percent and Time Left** and **Adapter Watts**. Every widget keeps one fixed width, so the text never moves the items beside it, and a value that cannot be read shows an en dash. A source refreshes only while its widget is visible.
- **Energy**: every readout runs from one shared schedule that sleeps while nothing is shown, while the Mac is asleep or locked, and slows down in Low Power Mode; it never polls while the hub is closed and no readout widget is visible.
- **Indicators** (Settings → Modules → Notch, an optional module that is off until you switch it on, needs no permission until you switch on Replace the system display, and costs nothing while it is off or while the hub is closed and every switch is off): short flashes in the closed notch, for the time you choose, over the part of the menu bar beside it that is free. On a bar with no room beside the notch a flash hangs just below it instead, at the same width. A flash does not show while the hub is open, and VoiceOver is told once per burst.
  - **Input source**: switching the keyboard layout or input method flashes its icon and its short code (the name too where there is room). **Show for** is 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 or 3 seconds (1.5 by default). Nothing shows while only one input source is enabled, and the module says so. holzBar learns of a switch from a system notification; it never polls.
  - **Volume and brightness**: a change of the default sound output's volume, the mute, or reaching either end of the range flashes the speaker and the level the output reports (or **Muted**), at a fixed width. The brightness keys flash the display's brightness the same way (see below). A change smaller than one sixty-fourth of the range never flashes. macOS still shows its own display unless you replace it.
  - **Replace the system display** (a sub row of Volume and brightness; off by default and kept on this Mac only): holzBar takes the volume up, volume down and mute keys and the brightness keys it can handle, and shows its own flash instead of macOS's display. It is the only place holzBar asks for **Accessibility** for this module; the switch is labelled as using a private interface. A volume key moves the default output by one sixteenth of the range (one sixty-fourth with ⌥ Option and ⇧ Shift held) and shows the level the output reads back. The brightness keys change the built-in display only while Display controls and its **Brightness** switch are on, and an external monitor only while Display controls and its **External monitors (DDC)** switch are on, through the two macOS interfaces that Apple does not document and that Display controls already uses; they never go below 2 percent and never write to a monitor that cannot be told apart from another. A key holzBar cannot handle reaches macOS unchanged: a full-screen app, a menu bar hidden by the system, a mirrored or shared screen, an output without a volume control, a monitor that does not answer DDC, the first three seconds after a wake, a key held with any modifier except ⌥ Option and ⇧ Shift together, and every other key. If the situation changes in the instant between a press and its handling (an output unplugged at that moment), that one press does nothing and the next one passes. If macOS takes the access away holzBar lets go of the keys at once, and if the private interface changes it switches itself off for the session and says so. Copy Diagnostics has an **Indicators** line with counts and fixed words only.
- **Gestures** (Settings → Modules → Notch → Notch hub, off by default): a downward scroll over the closed notch opens the hub and an upward one closes it; a sideways swipe skips a track through Now Playing and flashes **Next** or **Previous** for a second. One action per swipe, with a trackpad (natural scrolling on or off) and with a mouse wheel; a scroll that began outside the notch's area never acts. The scroll monitor listens only, needs no permission and exists only while the hub and the switch are on.
- **Follow the pointer** (**Show the hub on** in the Notch hub's settings): the hub opens on the display where the pointer is, with the hotkey, an alert click or an indicator; with the pointer on no display that has the hub, on the display of the key window (All displays opens on the first display then).
- **Pin the hub**: a pin button at the left end of the line under the notch (**Pin the hub** and **Unpin the hub**). A pinned hub stays open when the pointer leaves it and on a click outside, and says "Pinned by you". Esc (for a hub opened with its hotkey, the only opening that takes the keyboard), the hotkey, a change of Space or display, a gesture and Settings still close it, and closing unpins it.
- **Bounce** (Notch hub's settings, on by default): off, the hub opens, resizes and closes with a plain 0.3-second ease and no overshoot. Reduce Motion still wins.
- **Sound output** (Display tab): with two or more outputs the volume card's output name opens a list; a click sets the default output through Core Audio and the list shows what the system reports. Only the default output changes, never the output for system sounds.
- **After a wake**: Display controls sends no DDC request for three seconds after the Mac wakes or a display changes, so a monitor that is still starting up is not written to; its card shows the reading state meanwhile.
- **Software dimming** (Display tab): an external monitor whose brightness holzBar cannot change (an HDMI monitor without DDC, or any monitor while External monitors (DDC) is off) gets a dimming slider from 0 to 90 percent with a reset button, named **Dim** and the monitor's name for VoiceOver, and moving by 5 percent with the arrow keys and VoiceOver. It darkens that display with one dark layer that ignores the mouse and never takes the keyboard, is put back after a wake or a display change, and is gone at 0 percent and when holzBar quits. When the monitor starts answering DDC brightness or macOS can change it, its level is cleared, so the layer never outlasts the Dim slider. The layer shows in screenshots. The levels are kept per display on this Mac only and are never exported. With every level at 0 nothing runs.
- **Notifications**: with the hub closed, a new notification shows a bell and the number of unseen ones (1 to 9, then 9+) in the notch for **Display time** (3, 5, 10, 15 or 30 seconds; 5 by default), with no text at all. In the Notifications tab unseen rows have a dot, and a row opens to a card with its full text (plain text, up to a few lines) when the pointer rests on it, when it has keyboard focus and with the VoiceOver cursor. The tab opens on the newest unseen notification as a card, with a **+N** capsule for the unseen ones behind it; removing the card brings the next one forward. Everything stays in memory, as before.
- **Calendar**: a row with a place, invitees, a meeting link or notes has a chevron (**Show details** and **Hide details**) that opens it in place, one row at a time: the place, how many people are invited (never their names), the meeting service's name and three lines of notes, all plain text, only while the hub is open. **Countdown before a meeting** (Settings → Modules → Calendar, once Calendar access is granted; **Off**, 1, 5, 10 or 15 minutes; 5 by default): the closed notch counts down to the next timed meeting with a calendar symbol and the system-drawn countdown, amber for the last minute, gone at the start, and it never shows the title. While it is on, with the hub closed, holzBar holds only the start times of the next two meetings (it reads the calendar for the time until the end of tomorrow at the start, after a wake and when the calendar changes) and wakes at most twice per meeting; with the setting off, no access or no meeting ahead, nothing is read and nothing waits.
- **The closed notch's height** is measured from the screen: on a display with a notch whose menu bar is up to four points taller than the notch's safe area, the closed notch is as tall as the menu bar, so it meets the hardware notch.
- **Making room** (macOS 27; Settings → Layout → **Making room**; needs no permission of its own and reads only the item positions holzBar already reads; nothing runs while Layout and the holzBar Shelf are closed):
  - **Folded-items notice**: when macOS folds Visible apps behind its overflow button, Layout says **Folded away: Docker, Slack +2** (the first two names, then how many more) and, while the holzBar Shelf is open, the Shelf says the same in a row under its tiles. The notice shows in those two places only and never on the bar itself; only the Undo notice after a move appears under the menu bar. An info button explains it.
  - **Fit warning**: before macOS folds anything, the same notice says **N items do not fit beside the notch** (**in the menu bar** on a display without a notch) when the Visible apps are wider than the room beside the notch. The warning is an estimate from the widths holzBar has read; it can be early or late.
  - **Move N to Hidden**: one button in the notice moves some apps to Hidden, which can make room. They are the apps you use least while **Usage suggestions** is on, otherwise some apps, at most three at a time; the info lists their names. Apple's items, holzBar's own, items that cannot be hidden or moved and items controlled by a rule never move. A move is one Undo step: the notice at the menu bar, ⌘Z and the Undo button in Layout take it back. If an app cannot be moved, the notice says **Could not be moved: N** and stays. With nothing to move, it explains that and shows no button.
  - **Calm timing**: the notice appears only after its state has held for three seconds, never while you drag an item, while a display changes or the Mac wakes, while an app is still launching, or in the three seconds after a move.
  - **When the menu bar is full** (**Off**, **Ask** or **Automatic**; **Ask** by default): **Off** removes the Shelf row and everything holzBar does for it, and Layout still shows its notice. With **Ask**, the Shelf row has a chip, reached with Tab, Return or Space (Esc closes the Shelf).
  - With **Automatic**, holzBar makes the same move by itself, once macOS has folded Visible apps away and the bar has stayed folded across two reads five seconds apart. It waits a minute after holzBar starts or the Mac settles, never acts while Layout is open or on a guess, makes at most one move per episode (a new one needs thirty seconds without a fold) and stops after three moves in ten minutes. The move is one Undo step with a notice at the menu bar, and Layout's **Notices** group shows **holzBar moved N items to Hidden to make room.** with **Undo** and **Dismiss Notice**. The Shelf offers nothing in this mode.
- **Ask where a new app goes** (Settings → Layout → Making room; off by default): the first time an app puts an item in the menu bar, holzBar asks where it should go and remembers the answer. The question is a row of **Visible**, **Hidden** and **Always Hidden** (that one only while the section is on) and **Don't ask again**, in the **New apps** group of Layout, in the Shelf's notice row while the Shelf is used, and as the first row of the Notch hub's Overview, with a small dot on the Overview button, while it is not. An answer moves the app at once as one Undo step. Apple's items and holzBar's own are never asked about. A question waits for a quiet moment (a minute after start, ten seconds after the last new app so a login becomes one question, never during a drag or while the app launches) and is kept, as the app's bundle identifier only, until you answer it, so quitting loses none. **Don't ask again** is remembered on this Mac. With the switch off nothing watches for new apps and the stored questions are dropped.
- **Not shown by macOS** (macOS 27; Settings → Layout, after Making room): one row for every app whose item macOS does not show, with the reason and one link. The reasons are **Not in Applications** (the app runs from outside the Applications folder, so macOS cannot name its item; **Show in Finder**), **No app identifier** (a process without one, which macOS cannot name either; **Show in Finder**) and **May be off in Settings** (another app's item lies hidden under it; **Open System Settings**, which opens the Menu Bar pane). holzBar changes no System Settings preference. The group is absent when there is nothing to list, and the list is built when Layout opens and not stored. See the [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#apps-macos-does-not-show).
- **Readable menu bar** (Settings → Appearance → Moods, macOS 26 and later): a built-in mood with **Apply Mood** that gives the menu bar an opaque background in the light or dark look of your Mac, with a thin border while Increase Contrast is on. It is not applied until you apply it, can be chosen from the command palette and by an automation rule like any mood, and its name cannot be used for a mood of your own.
- **Spotlight** (Settings → Modules → Widgets): a menu bar widget, a magnifying glass, that opens Spotlight through the system when you click it. One copy, no text and no option.
- **Large Percent** (the Battery widget's **Format**): a battery symbol and the percentage in a larger size, at one fixed width, with a charging symbol while the Mac charges.
- **Import from Thaw and Hidden Bar** (Settings → Advanced, under **Back up settings**, the row **Import from Another App…**; needs no permission): bring over the settings of another menu bar manager without setting holzBar up again.
  - **Choosing the source**: the sheet lists **Thaw** and **Hidden Bar**, each marked **Found** or **Not found** by its app identifier. For a copy holzBar cannot see (a differently built app, or a settings file you exported), **Settings file** with **Choose File…** reads one settings file and recognises Thaw or Hidden Bar by the settings inside it. holzBar reads another app's preferences only after you click **Preview Import**, never by itself.
  - **The preview**: before anything changes, the sheet lists every setting that will change with its old and its new value (**name, from X to Y**), in the order of the Settings window, and says how many settings will change and how many other settings are ignored. A setting that holzBar already has with the same value is not listed. If nothing would change, the sheet says **Nothing to change**.
  - **What comes over**: only settings that holzBar has a matching setting for and that hold a valid value, such as the switches for showing on click, hover and scroll, auto rehide, the Always-Hidden section and the Shelf, the hover delay and the rehide times, the rehide strategy, and Thaw's hotkeys for the actions holzBar has. Numbers outside holzBar's range are brought into it, and the preview shows the value that will be written. Only the settings the source has are replaced; everything else in holzBar stays as it is.
  - **Not imported**: window sizes, item positions, separator positions, scripts and the login item are never imported. Thaw's menu bar appearance and Hidden Bar's global shortcut are listed under **Not imported** in the preview and are left alone.
  - **Backup and restart**: **Import Settings** first writes the settings holzBar has now to a backup file on this Mac (one file, `ImportBackup.plist` in `~/Library/Application Support/holzBar`, readable only by you and left out of backups), and only then changes them; if the backup cannot be written, nothing is imported and the sheet says so. holzBar then restarts to apply the settings.
  - **Undo**: after the restart a notice, **Imported N settings from Thaw** (or Hidden Bar), offers **Undo**, which puts the earlier settings back and restarts once more. The notice shows once. A **Last import** row in Settings → Advanced stays after that, with **Restore Settings Before Import…**: it asks first (Cancel is the default, **Restore and Restart** confirms), then puts back only the settings the import changed, deletes the backup and restarts. A later import replaces the backup. Every setting the import did not change stays as it is.
- **Dock badges** (Settings → Modules, under **Menu Bar**; an optional module that is off until you switch it on): the unread counts of apps in your Dock, as menu bar items. It needs **Accessibility**, which holzBar already asks for, and costs nothing while it is off.
  - **Apps in the Dock**: the module lists the apps in your Dock in the Dock's order, each with its icon and a switch. Switch on the ones you want; each becomes a menu bar item with the app's icon and the Dock's count in a red bubble on its corner (**99+** above 99; a badge that is text, such as a dot or a short word, shows as it is up to three characters, and a longer one shows its first two characters and an ellipsis). An app you chose that has left the Dock stays in the list as **Not in Dock**. Hovering an item and VoiceOver say the app's name and its count, or **No badge**.
  - **Read badge counts** (off by default and kept on this Mac only; labelled **private interface** in Settings): without it the items show the apps' icons and no numbers. With it, holzBar reads the badge of each chosen app's Dock icon through a private macOS interface that Apple does not document and that can stop working. If macOS changes it, holzBar switches the read off by itself and the module says **Badge counts are not available on this Mac**. The list of apps itself uses public Accessibility attributes only.
  - **When a count is zero**: **Dim** (the default) keeps the item in its place at half strength with no bubble, so the bar does not jump; **Hide** removes the item until the count is above zero. One setting for all apps.
  - **Groups** (**New Group…**): a group shows several chosen apps as one item, with the first two icons overlapped and one bubble with the total of their counts. ### Built-in items and app launchers

The **Widgets** module (Settings → Modules, off until you switch it on) also offers items that do one small job. Each is a menu bar item of holzBar's own, like a widget: it can be put in any section, hides with it, is kept in layout profiles and shows in the holzBar Shelf and the search, so it keeps working while macOS hides its own items. None needs a permission of its own, a network connection or a private framework, and nothing of them runs while the module is off. **Add Widget** lists them.

The module now holds up to twelve widgets, the readouts, buttons and scripts included.

A script runs only from the folder you choose (by default `~/Library/Application Support/holzBar/Scripts`, which is a real folder that only you can write to) and only after you allowed its exact content on a sheet that names the file, its folder, its size and the start of its checksum; the content is checked again before every run, so a changed file needs a new approval. There are no arguments and no shell, and the only thing a script is told is the event, in `HOLZBAR_EVENT`, which is one of six fixed values: `rule-started`, `rule-ended`, `check`, `profile-will-apply`, `profile-did-apply` and `widget-refresh` (a script widget's refresh). A script gets 10 seconds by default (1 to 60 in the pane), then it is stopped together with what it started; at most ten condition checks and ten other runs a minute (a run that is dropped for that, or because the script still runs, is shown as skipped), and a condition is asked at most every 30 seconds. The first line of its output is shown in the pane as plain text, in memory only. A script runs as you with holzBar's permissions, and macOS asks separately before a script controls another app. An allowed script may be able to use holzBar's Accessibility and Screen Recording itself, so treat it as being as powerful as holzBar. Scripts, their approvals and the rules that use them stay on this Mac in a file only you can read: they are never exported or imported, and an import says how many rules it left out. No URL command or Shortcut can turn on a rule that uses a script.
- **Profile hooks**: a script that runs before or after a profile is applied, for one profile or for every profile. Before hooks run first (at most five) and holzBar waits for them, each at most its time limit and all together at most a minute, then applies the profile whatever their outcome; after hooks start once the items have moved and are not waited for; applying another profile cancels one that is still waiting. A profile applied by a URL command, a Shortcut or the Focus filter runs no hooks. A hook needs an allowed script like any other, and a hook follows a renamed profile and goes with a deleted one (undoing a deletion brings the profile back without its hooks).
- **Widgets**: menu bar items of your own, an optional module in Settings, Modules that is off until you switch it on and costs nothing while it is off. On macOS 14 to 27.
  - **Seven sources, none needs a permission**: date and time, battery (percent, with or without the charging state), CPU load, memory (percent used or gigabytes used), uptime, free disk space (gigabytes or percent) and a countdown that only shows the time left until a moment you pick (no alarm, no sound, no pause). At most twelve widgets. Focus, AirDrop, media controls, fast user switching, a timer and app launchers are built-in items of the same module ([what they do](#built-in-items-and-app-launchers)).
  - **Formats are presets**, written in your language and region, with an optional symbol in front. Seconds exist for the clock only and the pane says what they cost: "Wakes the Mac every second while visible." A widget keeps one fixed width for its format, so the text never moves the items beside it; a value that cannot be read shows an en dash and VoiceOver says "unavailable".
  - **A Shortcut button** shows a title or a symbol and runs a Shortcut chosen by typing its name, through the Shortcuts app. holzBar shows nothing of its own, and a Shortcut that does not exist shows only the Shortcuts app's own error. A text widget has no click action.
  - **A script widget** shows the first line of the output of a script you allowed, cleaned to plain text (at most 80 characters) in a narrow, medium or wide fixed width with an optional symbol; a longer line is cut with an ellipsis, and a script that fails, times out, is empty or is not allowed shows an en dash. You choose the script by its name from your scripts folder, and it must be allowed in Automation, Scripts first: the pane says so and takes you there. It runs when the widget appears (a reveal, an unlock, the start of holzBar) and when you click it, and again on the system events that an enabled automation rule follows (such as power, apps, displays, the network, the Wi-Fi name, or camera and microphone use); with no such rule it runs only when it appears and on a click. It never runs on a timer and never while the widget is hidden, or while the Mac is asleep or locked, and at most every 30 seconds per widget (your own click or a new choice skips that wait) and ten widget runs a minute. The script is told `HOLZBAR_EVENT=widget-refresh` and nothing else, and runs under every rule of Scripts above. Which script a widget runs is kept on this Mac only: it is never exported, imported or shared, so an imported script widget says "Choose a script".
  - **Energy**: a widget refreshes only while it is visible, never while it is hidden, or while the Mac is asleep or locked; it refreshes once when it is shown again. The battery is read from power events, not by a timer, and Low Power Mode doubles the polling intervals (CPU load 5 s, memory 10 s, uptime 1 min, free disk space 5 min by default).
  - **Part of the bar like any item**: widgets can be moved between sections in Layout, are kept in layout profiles, and appear in the holzBar Shelf, the search, groups and hints (a Shortcut button runs from there too). On macOS 27 holzBar hides a widget itself, by collapsing it; "Show When It Changes" does nothing for a widget there.
  - **Settings export** carries the widgets without Shortcut names or scripts, so an imported button says "Choose a Shortcut" and an imported script widget says "Choose a script"; shared profiles never carry a widget. Each widget has a VoiceOver label and value.

**Safety and convenience**
- **Layout snapshots** taken automatically when the bar settles, once a day and before a profile rearranges it, with a preview and a one-click restore that can be undone; the newest ten kept by default (you can change the number), on this Mac only.
- **Share a profile** as a small validated file: only the applications you leave checked and their sections, no display, Wi-Fi, hotkey or other setting, and no code. Importing adds the profile to the list and moves nothing until you apply it. Double-clicking a `.holzbarprofile` in Finder is not set up yet; use Profile from a file in Settings → Profiles & History.
- **Touch ID or password** to show hidden items, in Showing & Hiding: hover, click, hotkeys, the Shelf, the search, URLs and Shortcuts ask once, then a minute of grace; your own safety rules (low battery, offline, rules, items you chose) do not ask. Turning it off asks too. A ⌘ Command-drag never asks: while the lock is on, it shows no hidden item. After you answer, the app you were using keeps the keyboard focus. It keeps a bystander away from your hidden items; it is not protection against someone who knows your password.
- **Revoke permissions in Settings**: a button per permission that withdraws it again (for holzBar's own entry only) or opens the right System Settings pane, so you never have to hunt for it.
- **Copy diagnostics**: in About, a report of versions and counts (and, on macOS 27, the state of its overflow button) you read before you copy it; no names of items, apps, profiles or networks, no paths; nothing is sent.
- **Tidy Up assistant**: proposes which visible items to hide from static facts only (system extras, the app's category, a short list of known helpers); live values, the clock, the battery and apps with messages stay. You decide item by item, the arrangement is kept as a snapshot first, and nothing is recorded. Any time from Layout; since 1.1.0 the introduction after the first launch offers it, and Layout's Suggestions group keeps it as one line with **View…** and **Not Now** instead of an alert.
- **Works without Screen Recording**: the Layout pane, the Shelf and the search show app icons instead of live pictures, with an offer to allow Screen Recording for the pictures (a confirmation in holzBar's own look asks once, the first time the Shelf opens; Settings → Advanced allows and revokes it).
- **System items pinned visible** (macOS 26 and earlier): profiles, restored layouts and rules leave the clock, battery, Wi-Fi, Control Center and sound where they are; you can still move them yourself.
- **Drag in the menu bar** (macOS 26 and 27): an optional module in Settings, Modules, off until you switch it on. Drag an app's item sideways to move it, with no ⌘ Command; on macOS 26 drag it past holzBar's divider into the hidden or always-hidden section. On macOS 27 drop it on the holzBar icon or on the open Shelf to hide its app (the Shelf draws an accent outline while the item is over it), or drag a hidden app's item elsewhere while it shows to make the app visible again. On macOS 26 every drop is kept like a move in Layout; on macOS 27 only a drop that hides or shows an app is, and a plain reorder is macOS's own. Copy Diagnostics names the last drop in plain words. A click opens the item's menu when you release it; Apple's own items, holzBar's items, ⌘, ⌃ and ⌥ clicks and double-clicks work as before. holzBar watches only the left mouse button, only while the module is on, with no permission beyond Accessibility.

**Polish**
- A [keyboard and VoiceOver checklist](accessibility-checklist.md) to run on a Mac; holzBar makes no accessibility claim until it was run.

## macOS 27

macOS 27 no longer draws menu bar items as separate windows — `MenuBarAgent` draws them all into one bar. holzBar ships a dedicated backend for it (from [jordanbaird/Ice#995](https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice/pull/995)):

- **Hiding** via assessment-mode assertions, with per-app sections
- **Item discovery** through Accessibility instead of window lists
- **holzBar Shelf** with real item images and even spacing
- **Layout editor** that assigns apps to sections — your old layout is carried over on first launch
- **A second display** works like the first: hovering and clicking items there opens their menus instead of revealing hidden items
- **Notched MacBooks**: holzBar's icon is kept out from under the notch, and items folded beside the notch come back on a wider display
- **No screenshots under the bar**: no screen-recording indicator when items are shown or hidden, and a click on the clock no longer flashes hidden items
- **Paired shape** (the former split shape) that follows the icons, on every display
- **Menu bar styles**: Single, Paired, the Apple-logo shape, outlines and gradients as on macOS 26; Sections is not offered. See [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#menu-bar-styles)
- **macOS 27's own overflow button**: while macOS shows its folded items expanded beside the notch, the holzBar Shelf does not list them a second time; in any doubt it lists them as before. See [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#macos-27s-own-overflow-button)
- **Drag in the menu bar**: with the optional module on, drag an item without ⌘ to move it as macOS allows, or drop it on the holzBar icon or on the open Shelf to hide its app. See [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#drag-items-in-the-menu-bar)
- **⌘ Command-drag across the holzBar icon**: drag an item with ⌘ Command across the holzBar icon and its app is hidden (to the left) or shown (to the right), with Undo in Layout and in a notice under the menu bar. A reorder on the same side and a drop on the icon change no section, and rule-controlled, pinned and macOS's own items stay. A switch in Layout turns it off. See [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#command-drag-across-the-holzbar-icon)
- **Whole apps in Layout**: pointing at an item highlights the other items of its app, and a note says that macOS 27 hides whole apps
- **Apple Items**: hide the clock, Control Center, Wi-Fi or the battery, and keep an Apple item one click away with a replacement icon of holzBar's own. See [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#apple-items)
- **Stand-in icons**: an app that runs from outside `/Applications` and the system folders (such as a device management agent) loses its menu bar icon while holzBar hides anything, even in Visible. holzBar shows a stand-in icon in its place; a click opens the app's menu and hides the other items again when the menu closes. A switch in Layout turns it off. See [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#apps-that-launch-while-items-are-hidden)
- **A dot on holzBar's icon** while hidden items are concealed and an app uses the microphone (orange) or the camera (green), in place of Control Centre's indicator. On by default in Settings → General, with no permission; the icon appears for the dot even when the icon is set to **No icon**

**Known limitations on macOS 27**

- Layout sorts the items inside a section on macOS 27 with synthetic ⌘ Command-drags (see the [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#reordering-items-on-the-bar)); profiles and rules only assign sections, and the holzBar Shelf does not sort there. Dragging an item yourself outside Layout (with ⌘, or without it while **Drag in the Menu Bar** is on) is macOS's own reorder, which holzBar does not record; only a ⌘ Command-drag that crosses the holzBar icon sets a section (see the bullet above).
- Opening a system item (clock, battery, Wi-Fi) while hidden items are concealed adds ~150 ms.
- Items of processes without a bundle identifier, such as some script-based apps, stay hidden and are not listed in the holzBar Shelf while holzBar hides any app, because macOS 27 lets holzBar keep apps visible only by their bundle identifier (see the [macOS 27 notes](macos27.md#apps-that-launch-while-items-are-hidden)).
- **Control Centre's privacy indicator is hidden while items are hidden.** Its indicator for the camera, the microphone and screen recording is not shown while holzBar hides any item, including a system item you hid with Apple Items, and comes back while holzBar hides no item. holzBar's dot covers the microphone and the camera, not screen recording; the small green dot beside the clock still shows the camera. See [Permissions](privacy-and-permissions.md#permissions).
- Coming from 0.0.5 or earlier, macOS asks for Accessibility once more. If holzBar is stuck on the permissions window, click **Reset and Grant Again**.

## Planned next

**None of this is available yet.** Every release is published as numbered betas first. Plans can change; items marked *spike* are only built if a short test shows it works well and safely.

### After 1.2.0 (release not fixed yet)

**Nothing here is in 1.2.0.**

**Menu bar**
- **Restore layout automatically** on macOS 27: a setting in Layout that puts items back in your saved order when macOS or an app moves them.

**Automation**
- More **App Intents** for Shortcuts and Siri, and a **focus mode** hotkey that hides everything except chosen items.
- An optional **MCP server**, off by default, so AI assistants on your Mac can read and change the menu bar layout. Local only, never over the network.

**Convenience**
- **Smooth show and hide** of the bar itself on macOS 14, 15 and 27: macOS 27 animates its own bar, and macOS 14 and 15 keep the instant change. (macOS 26 has it since 0.0.11, see above; the holzBar Shelf fades in since 0.0.9.)

**Polish**
- A Control Center control to toggle Zen mode or apply a profile. *Spike, optional.*

### 1.3.0: launcher and tools

An app launcher and search panel, file search through Spotlight, a calculator and unit converter (no currencies, because that needs the network), an emoji picker (no GIFs), text snippets, quick actions for system commands and Shortcuts, a radial menu, a scratchpad, and a tool to end a process or the one using a port.

### Later (order open)

Window management (app switcher, Dock previews, layouts), mouse and keyboard tweaks (scroll direction, smooth scrolling, no acceleration, mouse button shortcuts, trackpad middle click, shortcut management), sound (per-app volume, microphone mute, music app blocker), energy and display (extra brightness, display tools, cleaning mode), capture (screenshots, text from the screen, screen and GIF recording, colour picker, measure, QR, camera preview), system tools (cleanup scan, app uninstaller, Homebrew manager, wallpaper tools, focus timer) and text tools with on-device AI (translation, dictation, grammar fix, agent and workflows).

### Principles for every module

Each module is optional and costs nothing while it is off. It asks for its permission only when you switch it on, and says why. No module uses the network. **All AI features run on your Mac with Apple's own on-device models.** Clipboard history, cloud features, weather, speed tests, fan control, a charge limit and a hosts-file blocker are not planned.

The comparison tables in [comparison.md](comparison.md) mark these with 🔜 and the release.
